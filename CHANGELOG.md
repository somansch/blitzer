# Changelog

All notable changes to this integration are documented here.

## v4.1.0

### Added
- **A route that follows the traffic: TomTom as a second routing service.** A start/destination route can be worked out by openrouteservice once, as before, or by TomTom on every poll, with the traffic of the moment - the route the traffic makes fastest right now, its travel time and how much of that is delay. The Blitzer.de search runs along that route, so what is reported is what lies on the way you would actually take, and the card draws it. Three new sensors per such entry: **Travel time** / **Fahrzeit** and **Traffic delay** / **Verzögerung**, in minutes, and **Route length** / **Streckenlänge**, in kilometres, with the time without traffic, the length and the moment of the last calculation as attributes on each. The card shows the travel time large under the title, and under it how much of that is traffic - *incl. +9 min traffic*, since TomTom's travel time already contains the delay - in green, yellow or red by two thresholds, 5 and 15 minutes unless set otherwise under **Layout → Traffic delay** / **Verzögerung**, with the route's name and its length right now - in the minimal format in its pop-up, right of the map and above the list; **Show the travel time** / **Fahrzeit anzeigen** switches it back to the subtitle line's *Route 38 min (+9)*. The minimal line itself gets a fourth part, the travel time, coloured by the same thresholds, with its symbol's, its text's and its fill's colour set per level under **Layout → Travel time on the line** / **Fahrzeit in der Zeile**; a tap opens the pop-up on everything. When the fastest way becomes a different one, the integration fires `blitzer_route_changed` with the new length, time and delay. The route's hours are set on its last screen - the days, and from when to when - and outside them the entry does not poll at all, neither TomTom nor Blitzer.de, until a refresh action asks: a commute needs no requests at night. A TomTom key is free, without a payment method, and good for 20,000 requests a month; the one the dashboard shows right after signing up covers the Routing, Geocoding and Reverse Geocoding APIs, the three used here. Editing such a route asks TomTom for the route again, so the screen says what the way takes right now. A way back created along with the route gets hours of its own, and a name of its own - **Name of the way back** / **Name des Rückwegs** on the same screen, filled with this entry's name and *return* added, which was the only name it could have. Picked on the first screen of a new route, **Routing service** / **Routen-Dienst**; an existing route switches under **Configure**, keeping its ends and via points. The stored route stays what the search runs along until the first live one arrives after a restart.
- **A scale bar on the map.** In the bottom left corner, in metres and kilometres or feet and miles as the instance measures, on the card's map and the pop-up's alike; **Show a scale bar** / **Maßstab anzeigen** under the map switch takes it off.
- **TomTom's requests are counted per month.** Each TomTom route has a diagnostic sensor **TomTom requests this month** / **TomTom-Abfragen diesen Monat**, and one more sensor counts all of them together, **TomTom requests this month, all entries** / **TomTom-Abfragen diesen Monat, alle Einträge** - the setup's lookups included, which have no entry yet: the figure to hold against the free 20,000. Every request that got an answer counts; the counts start at nought on the first of each month, keep the month before as `previous_month_requests`, and survive a restart.

### Changed
- **Controls and hazards are two switches on the card, not a dropdown.** Under the area dropdown, side by side; one of them off shows the other half, both off shows nothing and says so. The traffic flow's switch moved there too, under them, with its refresh beside it. The filter dropdown's look under **Layout** is gone with it - a card that had set one keeps working and drops those keys the next time it is saved - and **Layout → Switches** / **Schalter** dresses the three switches instead. `filter` and `show_filter_picker` keep their meaning.
- **The refresh buttons say how long ago.** The area's refresh and the traffic flow's are a soft grey pill now, in the theme's own shade, with the time of the last fetch in it - *just now*, *5 min ago* - which the subtitle line then no longer repeats. **Layout → Refresh buttons** / **Aktualisieren-Knöpfe** sets the words' colour and font, the pill and its colour, besides the symbol. **Offer the Traffic flow switch** / **Schalter für Verkehrsfluss anbieten** under **Content** takes the flow's switch and button away and leaves the flow drawn.
- **The minimal format's settings are two groups.** Under Settings → General, **Buttons** holds the line's four parts - *Total*, *New*, *Delay*, *Travel time*, each named for what it shows - and **Pop-up** what the pop-up behind them shows: the map with its rows, the list, the travel time. *Map click highlights the entry* stands under the map in every format now.
- **The minimal line's third part is the minutes alone.** *+21 min* without the word beside it, which did not fit on a card of ordinary width.
- **A closure for the other direction is no longer "on the route".** An official closure whose text names its direction - *A8 Vollsperrung AS Flughafen-Messe FR München*, *A3 Würzburg Richtung Frankfurt* - is drawn by Blitzer.de with its line running that way, and on a motorway the other carriageway lies well inside the distance from the route, so a long closure there counted as on the route whichever way it was for. Such a closure now has to run the route's way as well. One naming no direction is judged by its side: with traffic keeping right, the other carriageway lies to the left of the route, 20 m and more on a motorway, while an exit or a ramp on the route's own side lies to the right - so a line running that far to the left is out, whichever way its points happen to run, and a line on a single-carriageway road, within a few metres either side, stays. Measured on Munich to Stuttgart: the closed ramp at Stuttgart-Flughafen/Messe and the roadworks closure at Leipheim, both for the other direction, are out; the exits at Hanau on the A3 and on the AVUS, to the right, stay. A point report keeps its distance rule - a camera placed by a phone scatters 50 m without that being a side.

### Fixed
- **The traffic flow reaches a pop-up that shows the whole route.** On a route entry the flow is asked for the route's own box, cut to the view, rather than for the view as a whole: the minimal format's pop-up fits the whole route into its map, and that view was refused as too large, so its switch said *not yet* and its refresh did nothing.
- **A route of more than 100 km can be set up again.** Setting up a start/destination route longer than that - as the crow flies, between the two ends - stopped with "The route is too long for openrouteservice, or has too many points". The route was not: openrouteservice offers its alternatives only up to 100 km and refuses the request for them beyond, and the refusal was read as the route's. Such a route is now asked for as the one way there is, and the wizard goes on with it; up to 100 km it still offers up to three.

## v4.0.0

> **Important: every area or route that polled faster than once an hour polls once an hour after this update - both halves, once.** Through v3.3.2 a new entry polled once a minute, and an entry keeps the interval it was made with - so the new hourly default alone would never have reached the ones that exist, and those are the ones Blitzer.de blocks for asking too often. The update sets them to the default and says so in the log, naming the entry. An interval of 0 (on demand only) and one already longer than an hour are left as they are. Shorten it again under **Configure** / **Konfigurieren** if you must - the entry's **Request count** says what it costs. A new entry starts at that hourly default too, where it used to start at a minute - Blitzer.de blocks an address that asks too often, and a route of a few dozen points polled every minute was measured to get there within hours.

### Changed
- **A new entry keeps 20 reports per half, not 9.** Both limits - controls and hazards - start at 20 for an entry made from now on; an entry made earlier keeps the numbers stored with it.
- **A closed side road is no longer "on the route".** A report with a line of its own - roadworks, an official closure - counted as on a route as soon as its line came within the route's distance anywhere, and a closed road passing under the motorway does that at the bridge. Now such a line has to run along the route: about four times the distance from the route (240 m at the default 60 m), or half of its length for a short one such as a closed slip road, provided that one also heads the route's way - the short piece of a lane passing under the motorway does not. A tailback keeps its own rule, the direction it queues in. A waypoint route, which used to take everything its corridor found, applies the same rule to line reports, measured against its corridor width; its points stay as the corridor found them, since its straight lines leave the road on every bend. Measured on Munich to Frankfurt and on a route up the A9: a closed lane under the A3 at Erlangen and a state road crossing the A9 at Berg are out, a closed exit on the A3 at Hanau stays in.
- **The card's URL changes with the file, not with the version.** A beta or a hotfix copied in by hand used to keep running as the previous version until a hard reload nobody knows to do, since the browser caches the card under a URL that only changed with a release. The URL now follows the file's modification time, with the next restart.
- **The map follows a badge.** Clicking the NEW or the delay badge narrows the card to what it counted; the map now fits what is left - the one tailback the card lists is the one thing in view - and goes back to the route, or to the whole area, when the badge is switched off. It used to leave the map where it was, so on a route the tailback stayed a dot somewhere along a line that no longer had anything else on it.
- **Links open the rebuilt map.** The blueprint's tap-through and the README's examples point at `https://www.blitzer.de/karte/?poi=<id>` now, which opens the report on Blitzer.de's current map. The old link form still answers, on the map that was replaced. A police report stays without a link: the new map resolves none either.
- **`type_name` in `blitzer_new_control` is the entity's.** The event said "Mobile speedcamera" where the entity said "Speedcamera"; both carry Blitzer.de's own name now, and the installation stays where it was, in `type` and in the summary.
- **"Change the route" asks openrouteservice only for a change.** Walking the screens through with the start and the destination where they were used to work the route out again all the same, and offer the alternatives again. An end confirmed in place keeps the stored route.
- **The data comes from Blitzer.de's own site now, not from the content server it used before.** The Blitzer.de map was rebuilt in late 2026 and no longer reads the content server this integration had queried since the start; it reads its own site, and the integration follows it onto the official public site. Why it matters beyond keeping up: the old server blocks an address at the TCP level once it has been polled too often - the trouble behind this release's rate work - and the public site does not appear to, so an installation that had been cut off should work again. No entry needs changing; the switch is internal. Four things read differently from the site, and the attributes follow: a camera no longer carries a postcode, so `zip_code` is empty (kept, so a template reading it keeps working); `created` and `confirmed` keep their short form - a time for today, a date for any other day - and the full timestamp the site sends is beside them as new `created_at` / `confirmed_at` attributes; a fixed camera's "never confirmed" is an empty `confirmed` now rather than `01.01.1970`; and a red-light camera without a speed limit has `vmax` `0` rather than `/`, and no longer reads "0 km/h" on the card.
- **A route asks for cameras and hazards in one request.** When an entry polls both on the same schedule, a route search used to make two passes over its points - one for cameras, one for hazards - and so two requests per point. The Blitzer.de endpoint takes a mixed type list (the Blitzer.de map asks that way too), so it now makes one pass that asks for both and splits the answer apart afterwards, halving a route’s requests: a 42-point route drops from 84 requests a poll to 42. Only for routes, where each point’s box is a small square: a radius search still asks separately, because a busy area’s hazards would crowd its single ~500-entry answer and push cameras out of it. Cameras and hazards set to different intervals, or either one alone, are unaffected.
- **One server again.** v3.3.2 fell back to two further Blitzer.de servers when the usual one did not answer, taking them for mirrors. They are not: measured from ten vantage points in Germany, Europe and the USA, both answer *403 Forbidden* to everyone, so the fallback only added two refused requests to every poll. The integration asks the one server alone again, with the 15-second limit per request kept. What looked like an outage of that server that week was the server dropping one address - the one it had been polled from - while answering the rest of the world; a server that answers a ping but not a connection is saying no, not being down.
- **Archive data are gone.** Blitzer.de's rebuilt site no longer delivers its archive of removed cameras: a request for it answers with nothing, and the rebuilt map has no such layer. The **Archive data / Archivdaten** switch is gone from the setup and from **Configure**; an entry that had it on keeps its other settings and simply no longer sends that request, which was one more per poll. `type` no longer says `archive`, and the blueprint's installation-form list no longer offers it - an automation made from the blueprint with that form ticked should have the tick taken off.

### Added
- **The map stays when nothing is reported.** **Keep the map when nothing is reported** / **Karte auch ohne Meldungen zeigen** under the card's map switch, on by default: an area with nothing to report keeps its map, fitted to the circle it searches - drawn as a dashed line around its centre, or around the tracker it follows - and a route entry to its route. A commute that is empty most of the day, or an area watched for its traffic flow alone, is no longer a blank card; off takes the map down until something is reported, as before. This is the blank box of [#3](https://github.com/somansch/blitzer/issues/3), where a route entry with nothing to report lost its map and its route with it.
- **The traffic flow, on demand.** **Show the traffic flow** / **Verkehrsfluss anzeigen** under the card's map switch colours the roads by how traffic is moving on them, with the colours and the speed steps of Blitzer.de's own map. It is not polled - an area polls once an hour, and traffic is stale within minutes - so the card asks for the view it shows while it is on screen: one request per view, one more after a pan or a zoom, a fresh one every three minutes while the card stays visible, none while nobody looks. The integration snaps the view to a kilometre grid and keeps an answer for a minute and a half, so cards sharing a view share the request, and counts each one on the area's **Request count**. Measured on a 50 km box around Munich: 34 KB for 1072 stretches of road at the city zoom, 637 KB at street level. On a route entry only the route itself is coloured, in its direction of travel - the other carriageway and an interchange's ramps are told apart by heading, since Blitzer.de reports each carriageway as a line running the way its traffic drives. **Layout → Map** has the lines' width (decimals allowed, 1.5 px by default) and opacity (50 % by default); pieces of one road that meet end to end in the same colour are joined into one line, so a bend opens no seam, and on a route entry a hole of up to 400 m between two pieces of a road is bridged in the colour of the piece before it (an area's view is never complete anyway, and is left as delivered); since Blitzer.de thins its answer out with the zoom, the integration asks for up to two zoom steps more detail than the map shows, within a budget of about 1.5 MB. The flow lies on top of the route line, so a thin band stays visible on the route. A line under the card's subtitle, and under the title of the minimal format's pop-up, carries a switch to show or hide the flow for the moment, says when it was last fetched, and has a refresh button of its own. Home Assistant 2026.10 or newer; the editor greys the switch out on older versions. The pop-up of the minimal format gets it too.
- **Official road closures carry the road they close and when they end.** A closure from the Mobilithek - Germany's public mobility data, which Blitzer.de files beside the community's reports - used to be a point with a text. It brings more than that: the stretch of road, a period and an origin, and the integration now keeps them. The hazard entity has `closure_since`, `closure_until`, `closure_open_end`, `closure_origin`, `closure_source` and the encoded `closure_polyline`; its summary stays the one line it was, the period is on the chips. The card draws the stretch as a red band along the road under the closure's symbol, the way Blitzer.de's own map does - in the card and in the pop-up, following every filter the symbols follow, switched by **Draw closures along the road** / **Sperrungen entlang der Straße einzeichnen** - and shows **Since …** / **Until …** (or **No end date**) as chips on the entry, switched under Layout → List like the other chips. Most of these closures are roadworks, which is why their text says "Baustelle": Blitzer.de files them as closures, and so does the card. A Mobilithek closure carries no address, only its text, so the road and the county are read off that text where it has a recognisable shape - the entry is named after its road, lists "NEA19 zwischen Hagenbüchach und B8 in Bräuersdorf, Kreis Neustadt an der Aisch-Bad Windsheim" as its place and keeps the rest of the text as its note. The chips' dates read `13.07.2026`, the way the reported and confirmed chips do, and a closure Blitzer.de knows no start of - which it dates `1970-01-01` - says nothing rather than "Since 01.01.1970".
- **Grouping nearby reports is a choice, and so is a group's shape.** **Group nearby reports** / **Nahe Meldungen zusammenfassen** under Layout → Map, on by default as the map itself is, gathers reports that lie close together into one group; switched off, every camera and hazard is drawn where it is, however many overlap - which is what a dense city area or a long route can want when the groups hide more than they tell. Home Assistant 2026.10 draws such a group as a bubble with the first few symbols and a "+N"; **Shape of a group** / **Form der Gruppe** lets a card keep that, or have the disc with the count on it that every version before drew (`cluster_style: disc`) - it sits exactly where the group is and a click on it zooms in to its reports, as it always did, with the same colour and font settings as the bubble. Both need Home Assistant 2026.10: older versions group regardless and draw the disc regardless, so the editor greys the two out there, with the tooltip saying why, rather than hiding them from anyone who read about them here.
- **One action that refreshes both halves in one pass.** `blitzer.refresh` - **Refresh** / **Aktualisieren** - fetches an area's controls and hazards at once and returns both lists. On a route it goes over the points a single time for both, so it costs one request per point where `refresh_controls` followed by `refresh_hazards` costs two: a 41-point route refreshed from the card used to send 82 requests and now sends 41. The card's refresh button uses it whenever both halves are shown; the two single-half actions stay as they are for an automation that wants only one.
- **Two counters per area, and a button that zeroes them.** Under the area’s Diagnostics: **Request count**, every request sent to Blitzer.de for this area since the last reset, cluster look-ups included, and **Delivered reports**, every report Blitzer.de handed back, counted before the area’s own filters thin them out. Together they say what a poll costs and what it brings - the numbers behind choosing an interval and a corridor width. Both survive a restart and only ever go up, until **Reset counters**, in the device page’s Configuration block beside the two *new for* windows, brings them back to zero.

### Fixed
- **No more "Cannot read properties of null (reading 'getZoom')" in the log on Home Assistant 2026.9.** 2026.9 draws the streets under a map through a Leaflet plugin that finishes a resize or a zoom in the next frame, whether or not the map is still there by then - and a pop-up closing, a view switch or the card rebuilding its map takes the map away in between, which the frontend then reports into the log as that error. The card now has Leaflet drop such a frame for a map that has been taken down; 2026.10's map engine does not have the race.
- **The pop-up's list follows the pop-up's map.** With **List follows the map** on, the minimal format's pop-up used to list every report whatever its map showed; now it lists what is inside the map's visible part, like the card does, and says "nothing in this part of the map" when the view is empty. The switch is offered for the minimal format for that. And the total at nought is pressable while the map is set to stay, so the pop-up with the area's map, and the traffic flow on it, is reachable from an empty line.
- **The card works with Home Assistant 2026.10's new map.** 2026.10 rebuilt `ha-map` on a map engine - MapLibre GL, with Leaflet only as the fallback - and the handles the card used to reach the map through went with it. On 2026.10 the route was no longer drawn, a click on a marker no longer picked its entry out of the list, a click on the map no longer let the highlight go, **List follows the map** and **Measure distance from: the map** no longer followed anything, the soft edge was gone, and the grouped-reports colours did nothing because a group is no longer a disc with a count but a bubble with the first few symbols and a "+N". All of it works again, on both of 2026.10's engines and on every version before, through whichever handles the map offers - the 2026.10 ones being the map's own `fitBounds`, `containsLocation`, `getView` and `map-clicked`. The group colour now lands on the bubble and its tail and the number colour and font on the "+N", which is why the editor's **Disc color** is now **Group color** / **Farbe der Gruppe**. The route preview in a route entry's setup is fixed the same way. A report on the map shows the pointing hand again, where 2026.10 had shown the map's grabbing one. And a click on a grouped bubble zooms in to its reports again wherever on the bubble it lands - 2026.10 zoomed only from the bubble's edge and its "+N", and opened the details of whichever symbol was hit instead. One more 2026.10 trap closed: its map reports a "move" whenever it is resized, moved or not, and a card redrawing itself can resize its map - with "List follows the map" on, clicking a badge sent card and map round in a loop until the page froze and its buttons went dead. The card now redraws only for a view that actually changed. And a map whose engine takes longer than a dozen seconds to appear - right after a restart, on a slow box - is picked up when it does, rather than staying without route and viewport for good.

## v3.3.2

### Fixed
- **An outage of one Blitzer.de server no longer stops the data.** The integration asked a single server - the one the Blitzer.de map itself uses - and when that went down, every entry failed until it came back. Two more servers answer the same requests with the same data, so a request that fails now moves on to the second and then the third, and only fails once none of the three answered. Whichever answered last is asked first the next time, for all entries.
- **A server that does not answer is given up on after 15 seconds.** There was no limit of its own before, so a server that swallowed the connection instead of refusing it could hold a poll for five minutes.

## v3.3.1

### Fixed
- **A card that registered a moment too early is no longer lost.** Home Assistant's app bundle replaces the browser's custom element registry with its own scoped one, as its very first statement - and that replacement answers only for what was defined into it. Anything registered before it arrived is invisible afterwards: `customElements.get()` returns nothing, `whenDefined()` never fires, and the dashboard shows *Configuration error* where the card belongs, with nothing in the console and nothing in the log to say why. Whether the card runs before or after that statement is an unordered race between two imports in the same page, which is exactly why it happened sometimes, and mostly after a restart ([home-assistant/frontend#53890](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/issues/53890)). The card now remembers which registry took its two elements and defines them again whenever that registry is replaced, for the first half-minute of a page's life. Measured in the browser with the registry swapped out from under it: both elements gone, and both back within 800 ms.
- **The card is listed under Settings → Dashboards → Resources now, and loads from there as well.** It has always been handed to every dashboard through Home Assistant's own extra module URL, which is written into a page at the moment that page is served - so a dashboard opened in the seconds while Home Assistant is still starting was served without it, and showed *Configuration error* where the card belongs until it was reloaded. That is the one failure the card loader cannot heal, because the loader is what never arrived. The integration now keeps an entry in the dashboards' own resource list as well. It is written while the integration sets up, and from the next start onwards it is simply there: the frontend fetches it for itself once it has connected, whether or not the integration is up by the time a page goes out. It is the same URL in both places, so a browser fetches and runs one card rather than two - measured on the test instance: one request for the loader, one for the card. The entry follows the version actually being served, a second one of ours is cleaned up rather than added to, and it is removed again with the last area of the integration. Nothing to set up either way; on dashboards in YAML mode, where the resource list is the user's own file and cannot be written to, everything stays exactly as it was.

## v3.3.0

Field and button names are given as **English / Deutsch**, matching whichever language your Home Assistant is set to.

### Added
- **What the jams cost, on the card.** Where the reports on a card carry a delay, a red badge beside the NEW one adds them up - *+10 min*, *+1 h 35 min* - and clicking it narrows the card, the list and the map to exactly those reports. Clicking it again, or the NEW badge, lets go of the filter. It counts the same reports as the number beside it, so with the list following the map it answers for what is on screen. In the minimal format the same figure is a third part of the line, after the total and the new count and in the same red, and a tap on it opens the pop-up on those reports alone; it stays there at *0 min*, quiet, so the line does not gain and lose a third of itself as jams come and go. On a narrow card the words go first and then the type steps down, so the figure is never cut off. On unless **Show what the jams cost** / **Zeigen, was die Staus kosten** is switched off, and styled through a block of its own - one for the badge, one for the part of the line - like the NEW marker.
- **A fourth search mode: Route (start/destination) / Route (Start/Ziel).** The waypoint route searches along straight lines between points you place, so it needs one on every bend and still cuts corners the road does not. This one leaves the road to openrouteservice: a start, a destination and optional via points go in, a route along the actual roads comes out, and it is stored with the entry and searched like a waypoint route - same switches, same intervals.

  Each end is a **zone**, a **point on the map** or an **address**, picked from a menu. Addresses are resolved by openrouteservice's geocoder, biased towards your home, and a screen of their own - **Address found** / **Adresse gefunden** - shows the match in an editable field before it is taken: continue unchanged to take it, correct it to have it looked up again. **Via points** / **Via-Punkte** pin the route to a road the fastest one would not take, up to 20 of them. Each one only snaps to a road heading the way the route runs through it: without that, a via point on a motorway could land on the carriageway running the other way, and the route would drive on to the next junction, turn and come back - the same stretch twice, out and back. The last screen shows the route on Home Assistant's own map, start, destination and via points marked, with its length and driving time, above the usual settings.

  There is **no corridor width to set**. The route is searched in boxes whose width follows from its length - about 40 of them, between 300 m and 5 km - because each box is a Blitzer.de request on every poll: a fixed 300 m would cost a 200 km route some 670 requests per half. The boxes are placed every box-width along the route, not one per route point - a route openrouteservice works out keeps a point on every bend, 782 from Munich to Frankfurt, and 80 boxes cover that. Where the distance below is wide and the route short, the boxes grow just enough that nothing the distance would keep can fall between two of them. The width in use is the new `corridor_width` attribute of the entry's **Total count** sensor.

  **Only reports on the route itself are kept.** The boxes find candidates; **Distance from the route** / **Abstand zur Route**, 60 m by default and adjustable from 10 to 300 m, decides what is reported, measured from the route line - and before the count limit, so the limit is not spent two streets away. On a first real route that kept 9 of 18 reports: the other 9 sat 290 to 800 m off the road, and nothing lay between 80 and 290 m. Roadworks that come with a line of their own, and tailbacks with an end point, are measured along their length, so a queue whose far end reaches onto the route counts. A tailback must also run the way the route does: on a motorway the opposite carriageway is well within 60 m, and a queue there is one the route never meets. Blitzer.de does not say which way a queue runs, and its own map never reads the end point; routing along three real motorway queues in both directions settled it - from the reported point towards the end point was the direct way every time, 1.3 km against 23.7 km on the A3, 2.3 against 6.6 on the A8, 1.4 against 47.7 on the A9. Speed cameras carry no direction beyond free text, so a camera in a tunnel's other tube stays in. The measuring is done off the event loop and against only the stretch of route near each report: on Munich to Frankfurt, 6000 candidates against 782 route segments take 0.1 s instead of the 9 s a segment-by-segment comparison took. A setup form has no field that can draw a line, so the integration's frontend file places that map in the dialog itself; that leans on how Home Assistant builds its dialogs rather than on a documented interface, and a dialog it cannot reach shows the screen without the map.

  It needs a free **openrouteservice API key**, tried out on the first screen with a single lookup. **Save the key for further routes** / **API-Key für weitere Routen speichern** keeps it with the integration: every further route skips that screen, changing it changes it for all of them, and removing the last entry removes it. The field is never pre-filled, since a pre-filled field hands its value to the browser with the form. The key is used only while a route is set up or changed - one request for the route - and never while polling. The route is thinned out before it is stored: 715 points for 23 km through Munich become 86, none further than 10 m from the road, against a search corridor of hundreds of metres. openrouteservice is reached at `api.heigit.org`, where its API has lived since `api.openrouteservice.org` was switched off on 24 August 2026.

  Editing offers **Change the route** / **Route ändern**, **Edit search settings** / **Sucheinstellungen bearbeiten** - the saved route as it is, no request - and **Change the openrouteservice API key** / **openrouteservice-API-Key ändern**. When no route can be found, a screen says why - a point off the road, no road between the points, a route beyond openrouteservice's limits, a key it refuses - and continuing goes back to where it can be fixed.
- **Which way? / Welcher Weg?** Between a start and a destination without via points, openrouteservice is asked for up to three routes, and where it has more than one the wizard shows them - fastest first, each with its length and driving time, all of them on the map, the first as the route and the others dashed, numbered like the entries. A via point pins the way down, so with one the screen is skipped.
- **Also create the way back / Auch den Rückweg anlegen**, a switch on a new route's last screen: a second entry, destination to start, with a route of its own from openrouteservice - on a motorway the way back is the other carriageway, and a tailback on one is not one on the other. Named after the first entry with "return" / "Rückweg" added, same settings, independent from then on.
- **A repair when an entry keeps failing.** Three failed polls in a row - a tracker without a position, a tracker entity that is gone, a Blitzer.de that cannot be reached - raise a repair under **Settings → System → Repairs**, naming the entry and the last reason. It clears with the next poll that succeeds. One missed poll is a phone in a tunnel and stays in the log.
- **`distance` in the new-report events.** `blitzer_new_control` and `blitzer_new_hazard` carry the marker's own figure - from the area's centre, from the tracker, or on a route the way along it from its start - so an automation can say "at km 23" without a reference point of its own. The blueprint's `{{ distance }}` uses it when no **Measure distance from** is set, where it used to read `unknown`; its texts say what the figure is on a route.
- **The Blitzer card draws a route entry's route** - a start/destination route or the straight lines of a waypoint one - on its map, and in the minimal format's pop-up, underneath the reports and fitted into view when the card first draws it. **Show the route on the map** / **Route in der Karte einzeichnen**, indented under the card's map switch, turns it off; a **Route** block under **Layout → Map** sets the line's colour and width, and a **Grouped reports** / **Zusammengefasste Meldungen** block beside it recolours the discs the map gathers nearby reports into and sets the count on them - its colour, size, bold, italic, underline. The count stays centred on its disc at any size - Home Assistant centres a line twice the font height, which sinks a larger count. Those are `--blitzer-cluster-` custom properties like every other element's, inherited into the map's own stylesheet, so a theme can set them too. On a route entry the card's distances are always the way along the route from its start, whatever **Measure distance from** says - a straight line from the map's centre says nothing about where on the trip a report is - and the setting's tooltip says so. It asks the integration for the route once over a websocket command rather than reading an attribute: an attribute would be written to the recorder again with every poll. The device's model reads `Route`, and the line under the card's title calls it a route.

### Changed
- **A route's markers are measured from its start, along the route.** A marker's state on a route entry used to be the distance to the nearest waypoint - a number that said nothing about where on the trip the report was. It is now the way along the route from its start to where the report lies, so the markers read like the trip: this one at km 3, that one at km 23. Both route modes; areas and trackers measure as before.
- **The search modes carry an icon each** - 📍 area, 📱 tracker, 🧭 waypoints, 🛣️ start/destination - and the start/destination mode is starred, with a note under the list that it needs a free openrouteservice API key. The key screen itself now says how to get one: sign up at openrouteservice.org, confirm the e-mail, copy the **Basic Key** off the profile page.
- **A waypoint route asks Blitzer.de fewer times.** The points it searches around are now spaced one corridor width apart along the route as a whole, rather than at least one per waypoint - so a route drawn with waypoints closer together than its corridor width costs a fraction of the requests it did, while searching the same corridor.
- **Configure** on a start/destination route offers **Edit search settings (distance from the route, types, filters)** / **Sucheinstellungen bearbeiten (Abstand zur Route, Typen, Filter)** - its own entry rather than the waypoint route's, which names a corridor width this mode has none of.

### Fixed
- **Tooltips in the card editor that were cut off.** Every "i" in the editor floats its text above the icon, and opens leftward or downward where there is no room for it. How much room there is was measured against the browser window, and against a container only while that container was scrolling - so in a tab short enough to fit without a scrollbar it was measured against nothing at all, and whatever really cuts a tooltip cut it anyway: the edit dialog's form column, the panel's own rounded frame, or on a narrow screen the dialog's body. The text is now measured against every edge that can clip it - each ancestor whose overflow is not visible, whether or not it scrolls right now, counted from the "i" outwards and through the slots the dialog shows the form in - and it is pulled back into the editor's column by hand where the "i" itself sits at that column's edge, which is what a long switch label does to it on a narrow card. Where a tooltip fits on neither side of the icon, it takes the side that loses less. Measured in the edit dialog across both panels, all six tabs and every setting of all three formats, at the dialog's normal width and with the editor at 340 px and at 290 px: 1,284 placements, every one of them inside the column and inside what clips it - against 4 hanging over the edge at 340 px and 8 at 290 px before, and, on the first row of a tab that does not scroll, tooltips 124 to 172 px tall cut by 3 to 51 px at the panel's top edge.

### Added
- **A third search mode: Device tracker (radius) / Gerätetracker (Radius).** The same circle the area mode draws, except that its centre is wherever a device tracker is at the moment of the search rather than a point pinned on a map. One form: **Device tracker** / **Gerätetracker**, a **Radius (meters)** / **Radius (Meter)** between 50 and 20 000, and below those two the area form field for field - the same installation forms, the same seventeen control types, the same ten hazard types, the same two optional sections, both update intervals.

  Every poll resolves the position once, before anything is fetched, so the controls, the archive and the hazards of one poll are searched around the same point; an entry on a one-minute interval therefore searches the road being driven rather than the one it was set up on. Each marker's distance is measured from where the tracker was when that search was made rather than from the home zone, and both refresh actions drive it unchanged. A tracker that loses its fix while running leaves the entry up with its entities `unavailable` rather than reporting an empty area. The entry's device carries **Tracker** as its model, which is what the card prints on the line under its title.

### Fixed
- **The card sometimes never appeared, until the page was reloaded.** Home Assistant loads a bundled card by writing a bare `import()` for it into the page it serves. That import is awaited by nothing, caught by nothing, and made **exactly once per page** - measured: one request, and moving between dashboards never makes another, because that is an in-app navigation and the document stays. So a single fetch that did not arrive - Home Assistant restarting in that moment, a proxy hiccup, a phone losing the network while the page was building - left the card missing for as long as that page stayed open, however healthy the server got a second later. Only a reload cured it. Worse, the browser remembers a failed import per URL: measured, a second import of the same address, with the file back and answering `200`, failed again without sending a single request. What Home Assistant is handed now is `blitzer-card-loader.js`, a few hundred bytes whose own fetch can hardly fail, and it fetches `blitzer-card.js` itself - backing off 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 seconds, under a fresh address each time, and trying again whenever the tab is brought back to the front or the browser comes back online. It knocks twenty times, which that backoff spreads over about eight minutes, and then stops and waits for one of those two - a server that is not back by then is not going to be talked into it twice a minute. Nothing needs reloading when an attempt gets through: Home Assistant watches for the element being defined and rebuilds the card where it had shown "custom element doesn't exist" - measured, the card appeared two seconds after the file came back, in the same page. Reported in [#2](https://github.com/somansch/blitzer/issues/2).
- **A card that is not on disk no longer costs more than the card.** An incomplete download used to be found out only when a dashboard asked for the file. Setup now says so once, by name, in the log, and carries on with everything else - the areas, the sensors and the map markers never needed it. If the loader is the file that is missing, the card itself is handed over instead: a card that cannot retry still beats no card at all.
- **The refresh actions on an entry that is not running.** `blitzer.refresh_controls` and `blitzer.refresh_hazards` answered an entry that exists but has not started with a `KeyError` and a page of traceback. They now say which entry it is and why it is not running. That state was reachable before - an entry retrying setup after a first fetch that failed - and is easy to reach now: a tracker entry waits in it until its device reports a position, and an automation refreshing on a schedule would have filled the log while it waited.

## v3.1.0

Field and button names are given as **English / Deutsch**, matching whichever language your Home Assistant is set to.

### Added
- **A card of its own: the Blitzer card.** One list answering "what is on my way right now", per area or route - controls and hazards together, nearest first, with a map above them that can either mirror the list or drive it. Three formats: **portrait** stacks the map over the list, **landscape** puts them side by side, and **minimal** is a single line carrying two numbers - everything the area reports, and what of it is still new - with the reports themselves behind it in a pop-up. It picks the area on the card or is pinned to one, filters to controls or hazards, sorts by distance, newest or oldest, measures distance from the area's centre, from the map or from a person or zone, folds a long list after a number of entries you choose, and marks what is new. Some 190 settings across two panels - **Settings** for what it shows, **Layout** for how it is drawn - each with an **i** describing it, and the editor writes only what differs from the default, so a card configured entirely in the interface stays short in YAML. It needs nothing added to the integration: the reports are `geo_location` entities, the area names and their search mode come from the device registry, the "last updated" moment from the area's **Total count** sensor, and what counts as new from that area's own windows.
- **The card is part of the installation.** No file to copy into `www`, and no dashboard resource to register. The integration serves it from `/blitzer_static/blitzer-card.js` and hands it to every dashboard as an extra module, with its own version as a cache buster so a browser holding the previous card fetches the new one after an update rather than after a hard reload nobody thinks to do. That is an extra module URL rather than a Lovelace resource on purpose: a resource has to be written into the user's own resource list, which only storage-mode dashboards have and which would then have to be tidied away again on removal.
- **The blueprint is part of the installation too.** "Blitzer - Report Alerts" is copied into `config/blueprints/automation/blitzer/` when Home Assistant starts, so it is waiting under **Settings → Automations & Scenes → Blueprints** with nothing to import first. The folder is the integration's own: a copy that has fallen behind is replaced when a release changes the blueprint, which is how a fix reaches an installation at all. A copy imported from the README's link counts as the same blueprint - it is compared as data rather than as text, since Home Assistant rewrites an imported blueprint in its own formatting - so it is left alone unless what it says has actually changed.
- **A refresh button on the card**, at the end of the line that says when the area last looked. Worth having above all where the area polls on demand only - an update interval of `0`. On by default, and switchable, as are its symbol, colour and size.
- **Tap actions in the minimal format.** Home Assistant's own set - more-info, navigate, URL, an action, toggle, or nothing - through its own action picker, so what this card offers is what every other card offers, down to the wording. Left on **Default** a tap opens the pop-up, which is what the format is for.
- **Counts as new for** / **Als neu zählen für**, per area and per half. How long a report counts as new, in minutes, default 60. Two windows rather than one, for the same reason the counts and the polling intervals are split in two: a mobile camera set up this morning is still news hours later, while a tailback reported half an hour ago has usually cleared. `0` switches the marking off for that half rather than reporting a permanent zero. It is answered per entry and in one place only, so a dashboard's visibility condition, an automation, a template and three cards side by side cannot disagree about what "new" means.
- **Two number entities per area**, **Controls new for** / **Kontrollen neu für** and **Hazards new for** / **Gefahren neu für**. The same two windows, put where the sensors they govern are listed rather than only inside the options dialog. They are configuration entities, so they sit under the device's **Configuration** heading rather than among the readings. Changing either is the one change to an entry that does **not** rebuild it: no refetch, no entity churn, nothing but a number two sensors report.
- **`new` and `new_minutes` attributes on the count sensors.** Each half's sensor reports its own window and how many of its reports fall inside it. **Total count** carries `new_minutes_controls` and `new_minutes_hazards` always - the one place both windows can be read side by side - and `new` for both halves together whenever at least one of them is on.
- **132 CSS variables, one per layout setting.** Named `--blitzer-<element>-<css-property>` throughout: the element as the editor names its block, the property as CSS spells it, never abbreviated, so `--blitzer-row-line2-font-size` needs nothing looked up. A theme can therefore dress every one of these cards at once. Nothing changes look until one is set - an unset property falls back to the card's own stylesheet rather than to nothing - and the order of precedence is card setting, then variable, then the card's default, so a theme sets the house style and a single card can still depart from it. They are documented in full in the README.
- **The card is offered for an entity.** Picking any report, count or window in the card picker's **By entity** tab offers the Blitzer card for it, already pinned to the area that entity belongs to and with the area dropdown switched off - whoever picked something of München was looking at München, not at every configured area. Both are ordinary settings, so either can be undone in the editor.

### Changed
- **The blueprint moved inside the integration folder.** It now lives at `custom_components/blitzer/blueprints/automation/blitzer/report_alerts.yaml`, which is what lets it ship with the download at all - HACS installs the integration folder and nothing else. Its `source_url` follows, and an existing copy still pointing at the old path is corrected on the first start after the update, so its **Re-import** button keeps working. The README's import link points at the new path.

### Fixed

"Abstandskontrolle · 100 km/h" reads as what the camera enforces. The figure is dropped on that kind now; every other kind is unchanged.

## v3.0.0

Field and button names are given as **English / Deutsch**, matching whichever language your Home Assistant is set to.

### Added
- **Traffic hazards / Gefahren.** Everything Blitzer.de reports that is not a control can now become a `geo_location` entity of its own: tailbacks, accidents, temporary and permanent roadworks, obstacles, slippery roads, obstructed view, broken-down vehicles, road blocks and police reports. Two new config sections sit next to the control ones: **Hazards** / **Gefahren** and **Optional settings for hazards** / **Optionale Einstellungen Gefahren**. Each type is tickable on its own rather than being lumped into grouped categories. The types differ enough in how often they occur - grouping them would make "roadworks" and "accident" an all-or-nothing choice. Every type is off by default, so an existing entry keeps reporting exactly what it did before. The type names come from Blitzer.de's own map language files rather than being invented here: several of them carry a speed value and would otherwise read like speed limits.
- **Hazards are fetched in a request of their own**, never alongside the controls. The endpoint answers with at most around 500 markers, in no particular order. Sharing one request would let a busy area's roadworks silently push controls out of the result. Measured across the Ruhr area: the same box returned 162 controls when asked for them alone, and 101 of those same 162 once permanent roadworks shared the request. Route entries search for hazards the same way they search for controls, sampling along the corridor.
- **Hazards have their own map source**, `blitzer_<area>_hazards`. A map card can therefore show controls, hazards, or both, instead of being handed one mixed list it cannot separate again. Each hazard carries `type`, `type_name`, `reason` (a fully written-out bulletin on a police report), `counter`, address and Blitzer.de's own creation and confirmation stamps. The types that describe a stretch of road rather than a point also carry `length`, `duration`, `delay` and the coordinates of the far end.
- **`blitzer_new_hazard` event**, on the same terms as `blitzer_new_control`. It fires only for a hazard that was not in the previous poll, and never on the first fetch after a restart.
- **A blueprint, "Blitzer - Report Alerts".** One automation covering the two things people actually want. A **live alert** for every newly reported control or hazard, restricted to a time window. And a **digest** of everything currently reported, triggered at a time of day, when someone leaves a zone, by any entity you pick, or on demand. Both halves share one set of filters - areas, controls and/or hazards, what a control measures, how it is installed, hazard type, minimum speed limit, a radius around a zone or person. They also share one set of channels: mobile push tappable through to blitzer.de's map, the notification bell, a dashboard helper, text-to-speech, and your own actions.
- **Both events carry `summary` and `type_name`.** An automation can print one field instead of assembling six. It does not have to look the entity up to do it, which would be a race it can lose anyway: the entity is added moments before the event fires, and its state is not necessarily written yet.
- **Separate polling interval for hazards.** A tailback is stale within a minute; a permanent roadwork is still there next week. Each interval costs its own request. Controls and hazards therefore poll independently, each with its own **Update interval** / **Aktualisierungsintervall** (default 1 minute, `0` for manual only), its own count limit, whitelist and blacklist. One coordinator still drives both. It ticks at the shorter of the two intervals, and each half decides for itself whether its own has elapsed. An interval only gets a say in that tick if something is actually selected for it to fetch.
- **`blitzer.refresh_hazards` action** - **Refresh hazards** / **Gefahren aktualisieren**. The hazards' counterpart to `blitzer.refresh_controls`, returning the hazards found. Two actions rather than one with a parameter, so each polls exactly what it is named after and leaves the other half's data and timestamp untouched.
- **The count sensors are named in your language.** They used to be German for everyone. They now carry a translation key, so Home Assistant shows **Control count** / **Anzahl Kontrollen**, **Hazard count** / **Anzahl Gefahren** and **Total count** / **Anzahl Gesamt** depending on the language it is set to. Any language other than German falls back to English. The entity id is unaffected: it is generated once, at registration, and an existing installation keeps the id it already has.
- **Two more count sensors per entry.** "Anzahl Gefahren" mirrors the control count, capped at its own configured maximum and broken down per city. "Anzahl Gesamt" adds both together, and is the one place both polling moments can be read side by side.
- **`last_update` timestamps** on all three count sensors, as attributes. Each half's sensor reports when that half last fetched. "Anzahl Gesamt" additionally carries `last_update_controls` and `last_update_hazards`. They record the moment of a fetch that actually succeeded, so a failed poll leaves the previous moment standing rather than claiming data that never arrived.
- **A switch per kind of control, instead of four categories that hid them.** The control switches used to be one axis mixing two questions: how a control is installed, and what it measures. The second had no answer at all. A tunnel camera, both halves of a section control, a weight check, a height check, a dummy and a plain speed camera were all "fixed" - 98 of 127 in the test data. There are now two sections. **Controls** / **Kontrollen** keeps the installation form (mobile, trailer, fixed, archive). **Control types** / **Kontrollarten** adds seventeen switches for what is measured: speed, red light, red light & speed, section control, tunnel, distance, weight, height, lane, entrance, access, crosswalk, overtaking ban, police check, alcohol, dummy, unknown. A control is reported only when both axes say yes, and switching a kind off also stops it being requested at all. All kinds are on by default, so splitting the axis apart does not make an entry report less than it did. "Only section controls" is now something you can ask for: on a 50 km radius around Sankt Veit im Pongau it turns 148 controls into exactly the 12 section-control markers.
- **Archive data / Archivdaten as its own switch.** Blitzer.de keeps a second, separate set of records alongside its live data: archived speed cameras and distance checks. Its own map calls them "Archiv-Daten", handles them apart from the live ones and draws them as smaller secondary markers. The live data agrees they are not current. Their `info` is empty, so they carry no confirmation count, no quality and no comments, and "Only show confirmed" cannot filter them. Their ids never overlap the controls reported as current. About a quarter of them sit within 50 m of one that is - the archived predecessor of an installation still standing. They now have a switch of their own, off by default. They arrive as their own `type` (`archive`) with Blitzer.de's own archive symbol, so nothing can mistake them for a live control. Like the hazards they are fetched separately - the archived speed cameras alone fill an entire response around Berlin - and appended after the live controls, so the configured maximum is spent on current ones first.
- **`kind` attribute**, naming the control that is performed (`section_control`, `tunnel`, `redlight`, ...) next to `type` for how it is installed.
- **`type_name` attribute**, on controls and hazards alike. Blitzer.de's own wording for what the report is: "Blitzer im Tunnel", where `type` and `kind` between them can only say "fixed" and "speed". Taken from the map's language files rather than translated here, so it says what blitzer.de says. Entity attributes are stored once for everyone, so it follows the server language under **Settings → System → General**, not a single user's frontend language.
- **`summary` attribute**, on controls and hazards alike: the whole entity in one readable line. A card or a notification prints one field instead of assembling six. A tailback reads `Stauende · 1 km · 15 km/h · +4 min` and drops whichever figures Blitzer.de did not send. A traffic control centre report reads `Polizeimeldung — <its own message>`, and a fixed camera appends the note it carries in `info.desc`. Where the type name would leave out the more useful half, the line names the installation instead: `Mobiler Geschwindigkeitsblitzer` for a mobile one, `Anhängerblitzer` for one on a trailer. Blitzer.de reports both under the same type name as a fixed one. An archived report is labelled `Archiv · …` first, because otherwise it reads word for word like a control standing there today. Where it is - street and city - is part of the line too. That is what makes it useful on the types Blitzer.de says nothing else about. An accident and a broken-down vehicle arrive with `info` set to a bare `false`, and a day roadwork with every field of its `info` empty. Without the location, the line would read just "Unfall". Follows the server language, like `type_name`.
- **`created`, `confirmed` and `info` attributes on controls.** `created` and `confirmed` are Blitzer.de's own stamps as it sends them, and are not full timestamps: each is either a date or a time of day, never both. A fixed camera reports `01.01.1970` for `confirmed`, because it was never community-confirmed to begin with. `info` is the raw detail object, carried whole because it stays under 200 bytes and holds things nothing else exposes - a fixed camera's written-out `desc` ("Einbahnstr. in Rtg. Velbert, 7-17 Uhr 30 km/h, sonst 50 km/h"), and `label`, `quality` and `tags` on a mobile one. Hazards carry `info` too, alongside the fields lifted out of it.
- **`tailback_speed` and `delay_minutes` on hazards.** Blitzer.de sends a tailback's `length`, `duration` and `delay` as raw numbers. Its own map turns them into the two figures a person actually wants: the speed traffic is moving at (`length * 3600 / duration` km/h) and the delay in whole minutes (`ceil(delay / 60)`). Both are attributes now, computed the way the map computes them, so an automation need not repeat the arithmetic. A 1 km queue on the A100 taking 240 seconds reads as 15 km/h and 4 min. Suppressed on both kinds of roadwork for the reason the map suppresses them there: a roadwork's length describes the works, not a queue crawling through. The raw three are unchanged, and plenty of tailbacks arrive with all of them empty.

### Changed
- **Breaking: each half of an area gets its own map source.** An area used to publish a single source named `blitzer_<area>`. It now has to say which half it carries: controls publish **`blitzer_<area>_controls`**, hazards **`blitzer_<area>_hazards`**. A map card listing the old name has to be updated.
- **Breaking: the two halves are named symmetrically everywhere.** The control half had been the unmarked one throughout - it was the only half there was for years - and that had started to make the rest harder to reason about. Renamed, all of it: the action `blitzer.refresh` is now **`blitzer.refresh_controls`** and its response key `cameras` is now **`controls`**; the event `blitzer_new_camera` is now **`blitzer_new_control`**. Automations and templates using the old names have to be updated. There is no alias, because leaving one behind is exactly the kind of legacy that prompted this.
- **Breaking: entities are identified by the config entry rather than by its display name.** This fixes a real bug on the way. Every unique id used to contain the display name, so renaming an entry changed all of them at once, and Home Assistant saw a completely new set of entities while the old ones stayed behind as orphans. Renaming an entry is safe now. Existing entities are migrated automatically, at config entry version 6. The count sensors keep their history and get tidier ids. The map markers are dropped and recreated on the next poll, since they are created and removed as the data changes anyway.
- **Breaking: every entry gets a device, and entity names lost the "Blitzer.de" prefix.** The prefix was redundant, since the integration is already called that, and it ended up in every id (`sensor.blitzer_de_berlin_anzahl`, `geo_location.blitzer_berlin_a100`). Each area or route is now a device named after itself. Home Assistant composes the full name from the device name and a short entity name - "Berlin Anzahl Kontrollen", or "Berlin Control count" in English - and the ids read `sensor.berlin_anzahl_kontrollen`, `geo_location.berlin_kontrolle_<street>` and `geo_location.berlin_gefahr_<street>`. The device's **model** field carries what was previously visible nowhere: whether the entry searches a **Radius** or a chain of **Wegpunkte**. Everything an area produces now groups under one device card instead of scattering through the entity list.
- **"Blitzer" is now "Controls" / "Kontrollen" throughout.** The section was named after the minority of what it holds. By Blitzer.de's own wording, 14 of the 28 kinds of report on this side are a "...kontrolle" - weight, distance, lane, entrance, height, section, police - and only 9 a "...blitzer". The remaining five (dummy, pedestrian crosswalk, overtaking ban, unknown) are neither. Where the form had **Blitzer** and **Optionale Einstellungen**, it now has **Controls** / **Kontrollen**, **Control types** / **Kontrollarten**, **Hazards** / **Gefahren**, **Optional settings for controls** / **Optionale Einstellungen Kontrollen** and **Optional settings for hazards** / **Optionale Einstellungen Gefahren**. Field labels follow, as do the count sensor (**Control count** / **Anzahl Kontrollen** rather than plain "Anzahl") and the refresh action (**Refresh controls** / **Kontrollen aktualisieren**). So do the entity names: **"Blitzer Berlin A100" is now "Kontrolle Berlin A100"**, matching the "Gefahr Berlin A100" on the other half. English uses "Controls" and "Control types" instead of "Types", which is Blitzer.de's own English vocabulary ("Distance control", "Weight control"). Where a text describes the search as a whole rather than one half of it - the waypoint hint, the route options - it now says **Meldungen** / **reports**, the superordinate word. A route corridor is searched for hazards exactly as it is for controls, so naming only one of them there was simply wrong.
- **Breaking: `type` no longer reports `redlight`.** It now answers one question only: how the control is installed - `mobile`, `trailer`, `fixed` or `archive`. Red light is a control, not a mounting, and lives in the new `kind` attribute (`redlight`, or `redlight_speed` for a combined installation). A template testing `state_attr(..., 'type') == 'redlight'` has to read `kind` instead. Config entries migrate automatically to the split switches and keep reporting what they did, with one exception worth knowing. An entry set to red light **only** ("Fixed" off, "Red light" on) now reports the other fixed controls as well, because the kind axis is global rather than scoped to the fixed ones. Switch off the kinds you do not want there.
- **The example Markdown card lists both halves in one card.** It used to filter the hazards out, because it was built around a speed limit and a confirmation count that a tailback has neither of. It now reads `summary`, which both halves carry, so it no longer needs to know which one it is looking at. The free text after the dash moves to a smaller line of its own under an information icon. The report and confirmation stamps make up a third line, dropping themselves where Blitzer.de sent nothing to show, and ending in the confirmation stars. Those are skipped for the three kinds of report whose count is never a community rating: a fixed installation, an archived record and a police report. The address links to the report on blitzer.de's map, except on a police report, whose id that map cannot resolve. An English version sits next to the German one in the README.
- **The three switch groups are multi-select lists.** A form field per option cost about 75px of height each, so the seventeen control kinds alone came to roughly 1300px of scrolling. As one list each they take 700px, 420px and 180px - measured in the frontend. The collapsible section around each group is gone with them, since a single field carries its own heading. What is stored has not changed at all: every option is still written as a flag of its own, so nothing downstream had to be migrated.
- Each poll now logs, at debug level, how many of each half it fetched and which halves were due. That is the only way the two independent schedules are observable from outside.

### Fixed
- **About a third of all controls showed the wrong symbol.** The picture was built from the coarse category and the speed limit. Blitzer.de's own map picks by what the control actually is first, and falls back to a category only after that. So a combined red light and speed camera was drawn as a plain speed camera, a tunnel camera as a generic fixed one, and both halves of a section control as ordinary cameras. 41 of 127 controls in the test data disagreed with what blitzer.de shows for the very same one. The map's icon table is now ported faithfully, including its rule for what counts as a speed in an icon name, and all 127 agree.
- **A camera explicitly marked as not semi-stationary was reported as a trailer.** The check asked whether `info` carried a `partly_fixed` key at all, not what it said - and the API also sends `partly_fixed: "0"`. Six cameras across the sampled regions were categorised as trailers because of it. Both this and the `fixed` flag are now read by value, the way the map reads them.
- **One camera in range could take down an entire entry.** The "info" field is not always the object the code assumed. Alongside the dictionary a normal camera sends, the live endpoint also returns a bare `false`, an empty string, and - on the odd camera - an empty *list*. A mobile camera on the A10 near Sankt Michael im Lungau was sending exactly that, and being sent twice in the same response while it was at it. With **Nur bestätigte Kontrollen anzeigen** enabled (the default), the confirmation filter called `.get()` on that list. That is an `AttributeError` rather than a connection error, so it was not caught as a failed poll either - it aborted the update outright. Anyone configuring an area covering that stretch of Austrian motorway had an entry that simply stopped working. All four shapes are now read through one place that treats anything other than a dictionary as "no info".
- **A duplicate area name could still slip through.** v2.1.1 started claiming the entry name as a unique id, which refuses a second entry of the same name. But that only works against entries already carrying such an id, and every entry created before that release carries none. Those are precisely the ones already holding the entity IDs a duplicate would claim, so the case the check exists for was the one it missed. The stored names are now compared as well.
- **An entry with no control type selected sent a malformed request.** Unchecking all four categories produced an empty type list, which the API answers with 200 and a body that has nothing to do with what was asked. Worth fixing now that an entry can legitimately be about hazards alone: the control request is simply skipped instead.

## v2.1.1

### Fixed
- **The searched area was not the circle you drew on the map.** A radius in meters was turned into a degree offset by dividing by 100000 and applying the result to latitude and longitude alike. A degree of latitude is 111320m, not 100000m, and a degree of longitude is shorter still - by the cosine of the latitude. The queried box therefore came out 11% too tall and, at German latitudes, 24-35% too narrow: with a 5km radius near Cologne the search reached only 3.5km east and west, while pulling in cameras almost 6.2km to the north. Nothing filters by distance afterwards, so both the misses and the strays went straight into the entity list. Measured against the live API for a 5km radius, this now finds cameras that were inside the radius all along - five more in Hamburg, one more in Cologne - and stops reporting the ones that were never in it. Route entries benefit the same way, since the corridor width is a radius around each sample point.
- **Resolving a cluster asked for the wrong cameras.** When the API answers with a cluster rather than individual cameras, the integration zooms in and queries that spot again. It did so without passing on which camera types the entry actually wants, and fell back to a default that was itself malformed. `[TYPE_TRAILER + TYPE_MOBILE]` nests a list inside a list, which reaches the URL as the literal text `[ts, 0, 1, ...]` instead of `ts,0,1,...`. The API answers that with 200 and a shorter list rather than an error, so the loss was silent - the same box returned 2 cameras instead of 5 in a Berlin test. The types are now threaded through, and the default is built inside the function.
- **A second area with the same name came up empty.** Every entity an entry creates is identified by that entry's name. Two entries called the same thing therefore produced identical identifiers, and Home Assistant dropped the ones it could not tell apart. The new area appeared with no cameras and no sensor, explained only by a line in the log. The name is now claimed when it is typed, so a duplicate is refused right there with “already configured” instead of failing quietly several screens later.
- **Every area logged under the same name.** Entries carried no identifier of their own, so each one’s coordinator wrote its messages as `blitzer (None)` and there was no telling which area a message came from. New entries use the name they were set up under; existing ones fall back to it.

## v2.0.0

### Added
- **Configurable polling interval** ("Update interval", in minutes) for every area/route, replacing the previous fixed 60-second interval. Defaults to 1 minute (unchanged behavior); setting it to **0** disables automatic polling entirely for that entry.

### Changed

## v1.6.0

### Added
- New **Route (waypoints)** search mode, alongside the existing area/radius search. Choose "Suchart" when adding an entry. Draw the route as a chain of map-based waypoints - one screen per point, the same drag-the-map interaction as the radius picker - and set a corridor width. Cameras are searched along straight-line segments between waypoints, without any external routing engine. Points spaced one corridor width apart are sampled and queried, and the merged results deduplicated by camera id. A route's `geo_location` entities report distance to the nearest waypoint rather than a single area center.
- A route's waypoints have no upper limit. Editing a route via **Configure** now offers a choice up front. Either step through each saved waypoint to reposition or remove it, then append further new ones; or jump straight to the corridor width, camera types and optional settings without touching the waypoints at all. It no longer discards and redraws the whole route, and no longer forces you through every waypoint just to change an unrelated setting.
- Multi-step forms (waypoint entry/review, the search-mode picker) now show a "Next" button instead of the generic submit label, since more steps always follow.

### Changed
- **Breaking**: The whitelist option is now a comma-separated list of city names (same syntax as the blacklist, e.g. `Berlin,Potsdam`), matched case-insensitively, instead of a regex pattern. Existing entries still using the old default (`.*`) keep working unchanged (treated as "no filter"); anyone who configured an actual regex needs to replace it with a plain city list via **Configure**.

### Fixed
- Adding a new area or route showed a completely blank form on the second step. The location/map selector field had no explicit default, which the frontend only tolerates on a flow's very first step (`Selector location not supported in initial form data`). Every location field on a later step now gets an explicit default.
- Adding waypoints to a route silently stopped being possible after the 3rd one for anyone who didn't notice the "Add another waypoint" checkbox had defaulted to unchecked once the 2-waypoint minimum was reached; it now always defaults to checked.
- Clearing the whitelist or blacklist field back to empty didn't stick: the frontend strips an empty *optional* text field from the submitted data entirely, so the schema silently re-applied its default (the previously saved value) instead of actually saving empty. Both fields now use `description={"suggested_value": ...}` to pre-fill the current value instead of a hard schema `default=`, which is not re-applied on submit — and unlike making the fields `Required`, still allows submitting them empty in the first place. The whitelist field also no longer keeps displaying the legacy `.*` value on existing entries — it's now shown (and treated) as empty, matching what it actually means.
